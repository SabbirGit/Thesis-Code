The research

Making model reasoning part of the conversation.

Clinical decision support systems can surface useful predictions, but a recommendation is more meaningful when clinicians can also examine the reasoning behind it. This project focuses on comparing explainable approaches in that setting.

“A useful clinical model should do more than predict. Its reasoning should be open to examination.”

Guiding principle of the project
A comparison framework

Look beyond the prediction.

The comparison is organized around complementary questions. These are evaluation dimensions—not reported results.

01

Predictive performance

How well does each approach perform on its prediction task and evaluation data?

02

Explanation quality

Are the explanations understandable, consistent, and informative about model behavior?

03

Clinical relevance

Can the explanation connect model output to information that matters in a care context?

04

Practical trade-offs

What balance do the approaches offer between transparency, complexity, and usefulness?
